About

The full story.

Finance professional, software engineer, Bay Area native. Here's how those three things ended up in the same person.

Origin

I grew up in Silicon Valley — not as a metaphor, but literally. I was raised in the Bay Area during the era when "startup" was becoming a household word and the idea that a small team could build something used by millions wasn't yet a cliché. That environment seeps into you. It shapes how you think about problems, what you consider possible, and what kind of ambition feels normal.

Growing up there meant being adjacent to builders. It meant absorbing, almost by osmosis, a certain orientation toward craft and output — the sense that the best response to a problem is to make something that solves it. That instinct never left.

Finance

After school I joined J.P. Morgan, where I've spent four years working in wealth management with a focus on venture capital clients — funds, portfolio companies, founders, and investors. My specialty is distributions in kind: the complex, high-stakes process of transferring non-cash assets from funds to their limited partners.

It's a niche that sits at the intersection of legal, operational, and financial complexity — exactly the kind of problem I find interesting. Getting it right requires precision, clear communication across multiple parties, and the ability to hold a lot of moving pieces in your head at once.

The Technical Turn

I've always written code. But somewhere along the way, it stopped being a hobby and started being a serious practice. The turning point was building tools at work — first a Python webapp to automate the processing of distributions in kind, then a form prefill tool that eventually got deployed division-wide. Seeing software actually land in the hands of colleagues and change how they work every day was different from building things that only I ever used.

Since then I've kept building: a trivia game in Go, Advent of Code solutions in Rust, a custom static site generator in TypeScript, and this website itself. The languages and domains vary. The underlying drive is the same: I like making things that work.

What I'm After

I'm interested in roles and projects that sit at the boundary between finance and technology — places where understanding both deeply is a genuine advantage rather than a novelty. I'm not trying to leave finance for tech or tech for finance. I'm looking for the work that needs both.
